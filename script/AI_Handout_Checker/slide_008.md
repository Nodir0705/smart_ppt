Those marks are only as good as the reading behind them, so here the system second-guesses itself with a focused second look.
A quick first pass can miss a faint correction written below the line and mark a right answer wrong, but rescanning the whole page at high resolution would break the other reads, so it goes back only to the suspect rows.
First, it flags wrong handwritten blanks that look like a possible misread, and then it zooms in, rendering just those rows at higher resolution and reading them again.
Next comes the re-judge step, where the sharper reading is spliced in and the item regraded, but only if that new read is genuinely better.
Finally, it remembers each row as done, so later reruns never repeat the work.
On a real handout this recovered a dropped answer, lifting the score from twenty nine to thirty out of thirty three, and it costs nothing when there is nothing to fix, which pays off once a whole class goes through at once.
